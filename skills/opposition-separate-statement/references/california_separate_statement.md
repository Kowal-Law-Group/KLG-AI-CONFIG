# California Separate Statement (CRC 3.1350)

Background and format requirements for California Opposition Separate Statements to motions for summary judgment / summary adjudication.

## What it is

Under California Rules of Court 3.1350(d), the **moving party** must file a separate statement listing each Undisputed Material Fact (UMF) supporting the motion, with a citation to the supporting evidence for each.

Under CRC 3.1350(e), the **opposing party** must file a separate statement that:

1. Reproduces each UMF from the moving party's separate statement, in the same numerical order.
2. Indicates whether each UMF is "Disputed" or "Undisputed."
3. For disputed UMFs, briefly explains the dispute and cites the contradicting evidence.
4. May add Plaintiff's Additional Material Facts (AMFs) the opposing party contends are undisputed and material to defeating the motion.

Format requirement: 2-column table.
- LEFT column: Moving Party's UMF + Supporting Evidence.
- RIGHT column: Opposing Party's Response + Supporting Evidence.

## Structure of the deliverable

```
1. Caption page (case number, parties, judge, hearing date, etc.)
2. Introductory boilerplate ("Pursuant to CRC 3.1350(e), Plaintiff submits…")
3. UMF table:
     Header row: "Moving Party's Undisputed Material Facts" | "Opposing Party's Response"
     UMF 1 row | Plaintiff's response 1
     UMF 2 row | Plaintiff's response 2
     …
     UMF N row | Plaintiff's response N
4. (For motions seeking summary adjudication) Issue 1 through Issue M sections,
   each as a separate centered heading + 2-column table:
     Header row
     "Defendants hereby incorporate by reference UMFs X, Y, Z" | (Plaintiff's response)
5. Plaintiff's Additional Material Facts (AMFs):
     Header: "Plaintiff's AMFs and Supporting Evidence" | "Defendants' Response"
     AMF 1 row | (blank — Defendants haven't responded yet)
     …
     AMF K row | (blank)
6. Signature block, proof of service, service list
```

## When Issues sections are required

Per CRC 3.1350(b)–(d), if the motion seeks **summary adjudication of one or more issues** (in addition to or instead of summary judgment of the entire case), the moving party must structure their separate statement around those issues. Each issue gets its own subsection. The opposing party mirrors that structure.

A motion that seeks ONLY summary judgment (the whole case) typically has no Issues section — just the UMF table.

The Weinstein matter we built had 9 Issues (one per cause of action plus three liability theories).

## Combined responses

When two or more UMFs cover the same factual ground, the opposing party may combine their responses. Common patterns:

- "Plaintiff's response to UMFs 13–15 is combined." (with full response under UMF 13)
- "See combined response to UMF 51." (under UMF 52)

For the Word document, the convention this firm uses is: **duplicate the response text across all rows in the group** (rather than merging cells). This preserves row-by-row alignment with the moving party's row count.

## "Disputed" vs. "Undisputed" responses

The opposing party should NEVER respond with just "Disputed." A bare "Disputed" without explanation can result in the fact being deemed admitted. Each disputed response should:

1. State "Disputed" or "Disputed in part" or "Undisputed but immaterial."
2. Briefly state the basis for the dispute (one or two sentences).
3. Cite the evidence that creates the dispute (after a bold "Supporting Evidence:" heading).

The Notion-drafted responses follow this convention — copy them verbatim.

## Common patterns in opposing-party responses

- **"Undisputed"** — concedes the fact is true and material.
- **"Undisputed as to [X]; disputed as to [Y]"** — partially concedes, partially disputes.
- **"Undisputed as to date, location, and presenting complaint"** — narrows the concession.
- **"Disputed. [Evidence cited] shows that…"** — full dispute with affirmative evidence.
- **"Disputed as immaterial."** — argues the fact, even if true, doesn't bear on the issue.
- **"Undisputed but immaterial."** — concedes truth but contests relevance.
- **"Disputed as to the legal conclusion."** — disputes a fact that's actually a conclusion of law.

## Exhibit numbering

Two parallel numbering schemes typically appear in the same case:

1. **Moving party's Appendix of Exhibits** — sequential numbering (Ex. 1, Ex. 2, … Ex. 23) keyed to declarations like "Weiss Decl. ¶ 11" or "Skootsky Decl. ¶ 6i."
2. **Opposing party's compendium** — letter-based (Ex. A, B, C, … Z, AA, BB, etc.) keyed to declarations like "Stern Decl." or "Rosenberg Decl."

The Notion-drafted responses use the opposing party's compendium numbers. **Do not reconcile the schemes** in the Word document — that's a manual step before signing. Both schemes coexist, with each side citing their own compendium.

## Filing deadline

Under CCP 437c(b)(2), the opposition is due **20 days** before the hearing on the motion (with court days only — extensions for service method apply). The skill should warn if the user-provided deadline is within 7 days of today.

## Service requirements

Standard CCP 1010.6 e-service to all attorneys of record. The Word shell typically has a Proof of Service template at the end with placeholders for the date and signing person.
