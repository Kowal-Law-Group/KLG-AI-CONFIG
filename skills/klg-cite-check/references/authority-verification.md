# Authority Verification Protocol

This reference file contains the detailed heuristics for
detecting hallucinated citations and the cross-verification
protocol for checking citations against Westlaw source material.

## Hallucination Detection Heuristics

### Why This Matters

AI-generated legal citations are a known and serious risk.
ChatGPT, Claude, and other LLMs regularly fabricate plausible-
sounding case citations — correct-looking case names paired
with real reporter series but nonexistent volume/page
combinations. The risk is highest when:

- Citations originate from ChatGPT Deep Research memos
  compiled into the brief
- Citations were generated during AI-assisted drafting
- The brief cites authorities in an unfamiliar jurisdiction
  or specialized area of law

A single hallucinated citation in a filed brief can result in
sanctions, loss of credibility with the court, and malpractice
liability. This protocol is designed to catch them.

### Source Tracking

Before applying heuristics, identify the **provenance** of
each citation:

**Attorney-sourced:** The citation was in the original brief
draft, came from a case assessment or response plan based on
the attorney's own research, or was added by the attorney
during the drafting process. Lower hallucination risk (but
still verify format and good-law status).

**AI-sourced — Claude:** The citation was generated by Claude
during brief elevation, research compilation, or other skill
execution. Moderate risk — Claude is generally more conservative
about fabricating citations but can still make errors,
especially with pincites and parentheticals.

**AI-sourced — ChatGPT/Deep Research:** The citation came from
a ChatGPT Deep Research memo that was compiled into the brief
via the research compilation pipeline. Higher risk — ChatGPT
is known to hallucinate legal citations. These citations should
be treated as 🟡 UNCERTAIN at minimum unless Claude independently
confirms them.

**AI-sourced — Unknown:** The citation's origin cannot be
determined. Treat as 🟡 UNCERTAIN.

### Detection Heuristics

Apply these checks to every case citation:

#### 1. Reporter Series Plausibility

Check whether the reporter volume exists or is plausible:

**California reporters:**
- Cal.5th: Volumes 1–16+ (as of early 2025)
- Cal.App.5th: Volumes 1–120+ (as of early 2025)
- Cal.App.4th: Volumes 1–75 (ended ~2012)
- Cal.App.3d: Volumes 1–276 (ended ~1991)
- Cal.App.2d: Volumes 1–275 (ended ~1969)
- Cal.3d: Volumes 1–54 (ended ~1991)
- Cal.2d: Volumes 1–71 (ended ~1969)
- Cal.4th: Volumes 1–63 (ended ~2012)

A citation to "150 Cal.App.5th" is plausible; a citation to
"500 Cal.App.5th" is not (as of 2025). A citation to
"Cal.App.4th" with a year of 2020 is impossible (that series
ended before 2020).

**Federal reporters:**
- U.S.: Volumes 1–600+ (check against known range)
- F.3d: Volumes 1–100+ (check plausibility)
- F.4th: Volumes 1–120+ (as of early 2025)
- F.Supp.3d: Volumes 1–700+ (broad range)

#### 2. Year-Reporter Consistency

The year must be consistent with the reporter series:
- Cal.App.5th cases must be from ~2016 onward
- Cal.App.4th cases must be from ~1991–2012
- Cal.4th cases must be from ~1991–2012
- Cal.5th cases must be from ~2012 onward
- F.4th cases must be from ~2021 onward

A citation to a "2020" case in "Cal.App.4th" is a red flag.

#### 3. Page Number Plausibility

Most reporter volumes have 1000–1600 pages. A starting page
of "1" is rare (but exists for the first case in a volume).
Starting pages above 1600 are unusual for most reporters.
Round starting pages (100, 200, 500, 1000) are not inherently
suspicious but are worth a second look when combined with
other risk factors.

#### 4. Case Name Recognition

Claude should assess whether the case name matches known
authorities. Consider:
- Does Claude recognize the case name and associate it with
  the cited legal proposition?
- Does the case name match the reporter/year combination?
- Is the case name suspiciously generic (e.g., "Smith v.
  Superior Court" — could be real but there are dozens)?

#### 5. Holding Plausibility

Does the stated holding make sense given Claude's understanding
of the area of law? A citation that perfectly matches the
argument's needs with a holding that seems too tailored to the
specific facts may be fabricated. Real cases tend to have
nuance; fabricated ones tend to be suspiciously on point.

#### 6. Cross-Reference with Known Authorities

If the brief cites a case alongside well-known authorities in
the same area, and Claude recognizes the well-known cases but
not the flagged one, that increases suspicion. Real briefs
typically cite a mix of well-known and less-known authorities;
an entirely unrecognized case cited for a well-established
proposition is suspicious.

### Confidence Calibration

Claude should be honest about the limits of its training data.
Key principles:

- **Do not confirm a citation you are not sure about.** If
  Claude has any doubt, classify as 🟡 UNCERTAIN, not 🟢.
- **Partial recognition is not confirmation.** Recognizing
  a case name but not the specific volume/page means 🟡.
- **Absence of recognition is not proof of fabrication.** Many
  real cases are outside Claude's training data, especially
  recent decisions, unpublished opinions, and specialized
  areas. 🟡 is the right classification, not 🔴, unless there
  are affirmative indicators of fabrication.
- **Affirmative indicators of fabrication warrant 🔴:**
  impossible reporter/year combinations, volumes that don't
  exist, page numbers outside the reporter's range, or a
  case name Claude confidently knows doesn't exist.


## Westlaw Cross-Verification Protocol

### Preparing Westlaw Materials

Westlaw Find & Print output arrives as .doc files. Processing:

```bash
# Convert .doc to .docx
python /mnt/skills/public/docx/scripts/office/soffice.py \
  --headless --convert-to docx westlaw_output.doc
# Note: soffice.py outputs to root (/), copy to working dir
cp /westlaw_output.docx ./

# Extract plain text
pandoc westlaw_output.docx -t plain -o westlaw_text.txt
```

### Parsing Westlaw Output

Westlaw Find & Print typically formats each case with:
- Case name and citation in a header
- Court and date information
- Synopsis/headnotes
- Full opinion text with star pagination (*123, *124, etc.)

Split the text file into individual case segments. Each segment
starts with a case header. Use the case name and citation to
match against the brief's citation inventory.

### Verification Checklist Per Authority

For each citation, verify against the Westlaw text:

```
□ Case exists in Westlaw output
□ Full citation matches (reporter, volume, starting page)
□ Year matches
□ Court matches
□ Case addresses the legal issue cited
□ Holding supports the specific proposition
□ Pincite page contains relevant discussion (if star
  pagination available)
□ Quoted language matches (if parenthetical quote)
□ KeyCite status checked
```

### Holding Accuracy Assessment

When checking whether a case supports the proposition for
which it is cited, apply this framework:

**✅ ACCURATE if:**
- The case explicitly holds or states the cited proposition
- The holding is at the pincited page
- The characterization is fair and not misleading

**⚠️ CLOSE BUT IMPRECISE if:**
- The case discusses the topic but the brief overstates the
  breadth of the holding
- The case supports a related but not identical proposition
- The pincite is slightly off (adjacent page)
- The parenthetical captures the gist but uses different
  language than the court used
- The case is dictum rather than holding

**❌ INACCURATE if:**
- The case does not address the legal issue at all
- The case holds the opposite of what the brief claims
- The case is from a dissent but cited as the majority
- The brief attributes a holding from Case A to Case B
- The case has been overruled on the cited point

### KeyCite Assessment

Record and assess KeyCite status:

**No treatment / positive treatment:** Safe to cite. Note if
the case has been followed or cited approvingly — this
strengthens the citation.

**Distinguished (yellow flag):** Still good law for its core
holding. The distinguishing case found the facts sufficiently
different. Note the distinguishing case and assess whether the
distinction is relevant to the brief's use of the authority.
Usually safe to cite but consider adding a parenthetical
noting the scope of the holding.

**Criticized / questioned (yellow flag):** The authority's
reasoning has been questioned but not overruled. Assess
whether the criticism is directed at the specific proposition
for which the brief cites it. If so, consider whether to
address the criticism preemptively or find alternative
authority.

**Overruled / superseded (red flag):** CRITICAL. The authority
is no longer good law on the cited point. Must be removed or
replaced. If overruled on a different point, assess whether the
cited proposition survives. If superseded by statute, check
whether the statutory rule is consistent with the cited
proposition.

**Not found:** CRITICAL. The case does not appear in Westlaw.
This is the strongest indicator of hallucination. The citation
must be removed and replaced.


## Special Considerations

### Unpublished Opinions

Unpublished California opinions cannot be cited except in
limited circumstances. If the brief cites an unpublished
opinion:
1. Verify it exists in Westlaw
2. Confirm it is properly flagged as [UNPUBLISHED]
3. Assess whether it is "directly on point" with "no
   published authority" on the issue (per KLG standards)
4. If a published authority exists on the same point,
   recommend replacing the unpublished citation

### Recently Decided Cases

Cases decided after Claude's training data cutoff are
particularly difficult to verify from training data alone.
These should be classified as 🟡 UNCERTAIN regardless of
other factors, and prioritized for Westlaw verification.

### Authorities from Deep Research Pipeline

Citations that entered the brief through the KLG Deep Research
pipeline (ChatGPT → Comet → Notion → Research Compilation →
Brief) should be cross-referenced against the Research
Compilation's authority list. If the citation appeared in the
compilation's verified authority list (post-Westlaw), it has
already been verified at the existence level. Holding accuracy
should still be checked against the specific proposition in
the brief.

If the citation was NOT in the verified authority list but
appears in the brief, it may have been introduced during
drafting or elevation — treat as 🟡 UNCERTAIN.
