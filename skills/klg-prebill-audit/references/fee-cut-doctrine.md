# Fee-Cut Doctrine — why each flagged pattern gets reduced

Internal training reference for `klg-prebill-audit`. It explains the
legal basis for each scan category so supplemented narratives are
written to the standard a court actually applies.

> CITATION DISCIPLINE: Every case name, reporter cite, pinpoint, and
> year below carries a [VERIFY] flag. These are stated from general
> knowledge of California fee jurisprudence and have NOT been
> Westlaw-confirmed. Do not drop any of them into a filed fee motion,
> declaration, or brief without verifying the full citation and that
> it remains good law. The doctrine is reliable; the citation strings
> are not until checked.

## The framework: lodestar and reasonable hours

California fee awards run on the lodestar — reasonable hours times a
reasonable rate, subject to adjustment. The court does not credit hours
it cannot find reasonable, and the fee applicant bears the burden of
documenting them. Vague, padded, duplicative, or clerical time fails
that burden and comes out of the award.

- *Ketchum v. Moses* (2001) 24 Cal.4th 1122 [VERIFY] — lodestar method;
  central to anti-SLAPP fee awards, where reasonable hours are the
  battleground.
- *PLCM Group, Inc. v. Drexler* (2000) 22 Cal.4th 1084 [VERIFY] —
  reasonableness of hours and rate; trial court discretion over the
  fee determination.

## Block billing

Lumping multiple tasks into one time entry prevents the court from
testing the reasonableness of each task, so courts may reduce or strike
block-billed time.

- *Christian Research Institute v. Alnor* (2008) 165 Cal.App.4th 1315
  [VERIFY] — inflated, padded, or unreliable billing records justify a
  reduced award or outright denial; the court need not credit records
  it does not find credible.
- *Bell v. Vista Unified School Dist.* (2000) 82 Cal.App.4th 672
  [VERIFY] — block billing obscures and tends to inflate; trial courts
  have discretion to reduce it (commonly by a percentage haircut).

Supplement fix: split the block into separate entries, one task each,
with task-level time. Show what each task produced.

## Long entry, thin description

A long block with a narrative too generic to test ("continue working on
AOB," "attention to appeal," "research re protected activity") is the
high-exposure version of the vagueness problem. The applicant has not
carried the burden of showing the hours were reasonably spent.

Same authorities as block billing; the defect is the same (untestable
reasonableness), magnified by the hours at stake.

Supplement fix: itemize the discrete work and the product — which
issue researched, which section drafted, which authorities run down.

## Duplicate / near-duplicate entries

Identical or near-identical narratives on consecutive days, or repeated
by the same timekeeper, read as the same work billed more than once.
Even when the work is genuinely distinct, identical text invites the
inference of duplication and the cut that follows.

- *Christian Research Institute v. Alnor* [VERIFY] — padding and
  duplication undermine the credibility of the records as a whole.

Supplement fix: differentiate the narratives to show the work
progressed (Day 1: survey and outline; Day 2: draft section II.B), or
consolidate if it truly was one continuous task.

## Intra-firm conferencing / multiple timekeepers

Multiple attorneys billing the same internal meeting draws scrutiny;
the court asks whether more than one biller was necessary. Some
collaboration is reasonable and defensible — KLG co-authors major
briefs by design — but it must be justified, not assumed.

- *Ketchum v. Moses* / *PLCM* [VERIFY] — reasonableness of hours
  includes whether the staffing was reasonable.

Supplement fix: confirm the second attendee was necessary and the
narrative shows why; otherwise bill one timekeeper for the conference.

## Clerical / secretarial work

Purely clerical or ministerial tasks are overhead, not separately
compensable at a professional (attorney or paralegal) rate, regardless
of who performed them.

- *Missouri v. Jenkins* (1989) 491 U.S. 274 [VERIFY] — paralegal time
  is compensable, but purely clerical work is not billed at
  professional rates; it is part of overhead. Widely followed by
  California courts on fee motions.

Examples that draw the cut: e-filing, formatting, bookmarking,
uploading, paginating, calendaring, assembling binders, preparing a
memorandum of costs, proofs of service.

Fix: write off the time, reclassify as overhead, or — if the entry
actually reflects substantive work mislabeled — rewrite it to show the
substance.

## Conference / email without a stated subject

Communication entries with no "re" leave the client and the court
unable to see what was addressed. Cheap to fix, and the fix doubles as
client transparency.

Fix: add the subject ("telephone conference with co-counsel re
anti-SLAPP fee motion strategy").

## The general principle for supplements

Specificity defeats vagueness cuts. But the cure is real task detail,
not more words — a longer entry that is still generic is no better, and
a padded one is worse. Write what the work was and what it produced, in
the firm's plain, active voice, drawn only from what actually happened.
And keep an eye on the privilege line: detailed enough to justify the
time, not so detailed it hands the other side your strategy.
