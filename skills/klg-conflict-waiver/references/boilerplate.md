# Conflict Waiver Boilerplate Language

This file contains the standard language for KLG conflict waiver
letters. Claude must use this language as the baseline for every
conflict waiver. Case-specific customizations are noted where
they apply.

---

## Opening Paragraph

> You have requested that our law firm, Kowal Law Group, APC,
> represent both {CLIENT_1} and {CLIENT_2}, in the
> {lawsuit_or_appeal} mentioned above. Whenever an attorney has
> more than one client in the same {lawsuit_or_appeal}, there
> is a potential for conflicts. After discussing the case with
> you, it appears there are no conflicts now. But things may
> change, and parties may wish to pursue different strategies
> or objectives in the case. These and other things may create
> a potential for a future conflict.

**Customization:** For more than two clients, list all names
separated by commas with "and" before the last. Adjust "both"
to match the number of clients (e.g., remove "both" for three
or more). Use "lawsuit" for trial court matters, "appeal" for
appellate matters, "proceeding" for writ proceedings.

---

## Professional Conduct Paragraph

> The California Rules of Professional Conduct requires that I
> obtain your informed consent, in writing, that you are aware
> of the potential of future conflicts, and the consequences.
> In other words, while sharing an attorney holds out certain
> advantages to parties whose interests appear to be aligned,
> there may be reasons why it could be a bad idea. So I need to
> disclose to you the ways in which it might be a bad idea so
> you can consider them independently.

**No customization.** Use this language verbatim.

## Six Numbered Disclosures

### First: No Client Confidentiality Between Joint Clients

> First, you need to be aware that when two parties are jointly
> represented by the same attorney there is no client
> confidentiality between them. (Evid. Code, section 962.) That means
> that whatever the attorney learns about one client may be
> required to be disclosed to the other client.

**No customization.** Use verbatim. Note: the citation to
Evidence Code section 962 is mandatory.

### Second: Litigation and Settlement Strategy Conflicts

> Second, a common way that conflicts arise in these situations
> is when selecting litigation and settlement strategies. These
> strategies can impact different parties differently, and the
> parties may have different ideas about them. At this time, I
> am not aware of any information indicating there would be a
> conflict between your respective interests. But if such
> information surfaces suggesting a conflict concerning a
> settlement or litigation strategy, there may be a need for
> you to consult independent counsel.

**Customization point:** This is where case-specific conflict
scenarios should be woven in. If the user provides examples of
how conflicts might arise (e.g., different settlement
preferences between an individual and entity, insurance
allocation issues, guarantor vs. primary obligor), add a
sentence or two after the standard language illustrating the
specific scenario. Keep the tone measured and hypothetical —
use "could" and "might," not "will."

Example of customized version:

> Second, a common way that conflicts arise in these situations
> is when selecting litigation and settlement strategies. These
> strategies can impact different parties differently, and the
> parties may have different ideas about them. For example, in
> this case, [Client 1] as an individual may have different
> considerations regarding settlement than [Client 2] as a
> business entity, particularly if a proposed settlement would
> affect them differently. At this time, I am not aware of any
> information indicating there would be a conflict between your
> respective interests. But if such information surfaces
> suggesting a conflict concerning a settlement or litigation
> strategy, there may be a need for you to consult independent
> counsel.

### Third: Consequences of Future Adversity

> Third, you should be aware of what happens in the future if
> your interests do become adverse. The most immediate
> consequence of a conflict arising is that one or both of the
> clients may need to obtain new counsel. A more serious problem
> could occur if a lawsuit were to arise between the two
> clients. While we all hope that would never happen, if it did,
> the attorney (me) might have to testify about communications
> I had with one of you while I jointly represented you both.
> This kind of uncomfortable possibility would present a problem
> for me as the attorney because of the duty of loyalty I would
> owe to each client. This duty prevents joint representation
> without informed written consent even if the adversity is
> unrelated to the representation. (*Flatt v. Superior Court*
> (1994) 9 Cal.4th 275, 284.)

**No customization.** Use verbatim. The citation to *Flatt v.
Superior Court* is mandatory and the case name must be
italicized.

### Fourth: Custody of Original File

> Fourth, issues could arise regarding the custody of the
> original file. Our firm policy is not to maintain custody of
> the originals. You are encouraged to come to an agreement
> about which one of you will maintain any original documents
> that relate to this case. By signing this agreement, each of
> you agree that if our firm stops representing one of you, but
> continues to represent the other, the client we continue to
> represent will be entitled to maintain custody or control of
> the original file. The other party is entitled to a copy of
> Client Papers as defined in Rule 3-700(D) of the Rules of
> Professional Conduct.

**No customization.** Use verbatim.

### Fifth: Disqualification Risk

> Fifth, in the event of a dispute or conflict between any of
> the clients, there is a risk that the attorney may be
> disqualified from representing one or more of the clients or
> that it may otherwise be inappropriate for the attorney to
> continue with the joint representation absent written consent
> from each of the clients.

**No customization.** Use verbatim.

### Sixth: Insurance/Asset Allocation

> Sixth, if there is insufficient insurance or assets to cover
> the damages of each client, there may be disputes regarding
> how to allocate the insurance proceeds or assets between the
> clients.

**No customization.** Use verbatim.

## Consent Introduction

> Given there is currently no conflict of interest, we may
> jointly represent the clients in this matter, provided that
> the clients give informed consent in writing. Each client
> should feel free to consult with independent counsel before
> finalizing the client's decision to proceed with the joint
> representation, including whether to sign this conflict
> disclosure and waiver. As the attorney, I need to emphasize
> that each client remains free to seek independent counsel at
> any time even if the client decides to sign this consent.

**No customization.** Use verbatim.

---

## Waiver Transition

> With those disclosures having been made, please carefully
> consider consenting to the following waiver:

**No customization.** Use verbatim.

## Waiver Text

This section is indented from the left margin (use a left
indent of approximately 720 DXA / 0.5 inches).

> The attorney's current understanding is that each client
> desires to have the attorney jointly represent them in
> connection with the matter referenced above. By signing this
> Disclosure and Consent, each client expressly acknowledges
> that the client:
>
> (1) has carefully read and fully understands the disclosures
> described above;
>
> (2) has carefully considered all of the circumstances and
> potential conflicts described above;
>
> (3) has had the opportunity to consult with independent
> counsel regarding the disclosures and consent in this
> agreement; and
>
> (4) agrees to the joint representation of the clients by the
> attorney in connection with the referenced matter.

**No customization.** Use verbatim. Note: the original letters
contain a grammatical error ("each clients desires") — use the
corrected version above ("each client desires").

## Subject Line Format

The Re: line format depends on the matter type:

**For appellate matters (with both trial and appellate case numbers):**
> Re: *{Case Caption}*, {Court} case no. {trial case no.},
> Court of Appeal case no. {appellate case no.}

**For trial court matters:**
> Re: Informed Consent of and Waiver of Potential Conflicts
> of Interest in *{Case Caption}*, {Court} case no. {case no.}

**Formatting:** The case caption is italicized. Everything
else is regular text. The subject line uses the `Subject`
style which includes a hanging indent after the "Re:" tab.
