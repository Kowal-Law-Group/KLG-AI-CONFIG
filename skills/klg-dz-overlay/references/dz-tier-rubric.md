# DZ Tier Classification Rubric

This file defines how to classify a DZ-sourced matter as either
**DZ Preferred** or **DZ Standard**. The classification controls the
rate structure (`references/dz-rate-structure.md`) and feeds the
engagement letter.

The rubric is binary. There are no middle tiers, no on-the-fly
hybrids. Each matter is one or the other.

---

## Classification Rule

A matter is **DZ Preferred** when EITHER:

- (A) **Normal-timeline matter:** KLG is engaged before or within
  the original briefing deadline, with the ability to secure
  60-day extensions if needed, and the engagement window gives
  the firm's normal pipeline adequate runway (research, brief
  drafting, internal review).
- (B) **Interesting matter:** The case presents an issue aligned
  with KLG practice values — constitutional, civil-procedural,
  appealability, anti-SLAPP, structural error, judgment
  enforcement, or other matters where KLG has an editorial or
  doctrinal interest beyond billable work. Case-by-case at intake,
  not categorical.

A matter is **DZ Standard** when BOTH:

- The matter is urgent (must begin substantive work without
  normal-pipeline lead time) OR unusually difficult at intake
  (no clear path to a colorable issue without Tim's personal
  strategic involvement).
- The matter does not independently qualify as Preferred under
  the "interesting" prong.

**If both prongs apply** (matter is interesting AND urgent/difficult),
default to Preferred. KLG wants to encourage interesting matters,
and the relationship rate is the right vehicle for that.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 — Routine appeal, ample runway → Preferred

**Facts:** DZ refers a civil appeal at the notice-of-record stage.
The opening brief is due in 90 days; KLG can request and likely
obtain two 60-day extensions. The issues are standard
substantial-evidence and abuse-of-discretion challenges to a
trial court ruling. No constitutional or procedural-precedent
dimension.

**Classification:** Preferred. Prong (A) is satisfied — normal
timeline, normal pipeline applies. The matter is not "interesting"
in the editorial sense, but Preferred also applies to normal
matters as the relationship default.

### Example 2 — Compressed timeline, conventional issues → Standard

**Facts:** DZ refers a civil appeal at the AOB-due stage; the
brief is due in 28 days, prior counsel did not request an
extension, and the calendared oral argument date does not allow
for further continuance. Issues are conventional preservation,
SoR, and prejudice arguments.

**Classification:** Standard. The matter is urgent (cannot absorb
normal pipeline lead time), and it is not independently interesting.
Both Standard prongs are satisfied.

### Example 3 — Constitutional or appealability issue → Preferred

**Facts:** DZ refers a matter raising a Government Code §
68081 issue, an anti-SLAPP appealability question, or a novel
constitutional challenge to a state statute. The timeline is
normal — briefing has not yet begun, and KLG can engage with
normal pipeline runway.

**Classification:** Preferred. Prong (B) is satisfied — the
matter is independently interesting. Prong (A) is also satisfied
(normal timeline), making the classification doubly Preferred.

### Example 4 — Interesting issue on a short fuse → Preferred (with caveat)

**Facts:** DZ refers a petition for review on an interesting
constitutional issue, but the petition deadline is 10 days away,
the trial-court briefing is voluminous, and the issue requires
substantial research to frame correctly.

**Classification:** Preferred. The interesting prong wins when it
conflicts with the urgent/difficult prong, because KLG wants to
encourage interesting work and the relationship rate is the right
vehicle. The urgency and difficulty are absorbed as part of the
relationship investment in interesting matters.

**Caveat:** When this combination occurs frequently, it indicates
that "interesting matter" is being read too broadly. The rubric
should distinguish matters where the issue itself is interesting
(petition for review on a novel constitutional question) from
matters where the *posture* is interesting but the underlying
issue is conventional (petition for review because the client
wants one). The first qualifies as Preferred; the second does not.

### Example 5 — Petition for review on conventional issues → Standard

**Facts:** DZ refers a petition for review after an unpublished
Court of Appeal affirmance on substantial-evidence grounds. The
client wants to "go for broke." The legal questions presented
are conventional and the issue is not one KLG would otherwise
have flagged as worth Supreme Court attention.

**Classification:** Standard. Petitions for review and petitions
for rehearing are NOT categorically interesting. The fact that
the procedural posture is a petition does not, by itself, satisfy
prong (B). When the underlying issue is conventional, the matter
is Standard.

### Example 6 — High-difficulty matter with no clean path → Standard

**Facts:** DZ refers a matter that Tim initially declined as
having no clear path to a colorable issue. DZ has come back asking
KLG to take it anyway. The timeline is normal (briefing schedule
allows extensions), but Tim must personally invest substantial
strategic time at intake to identify and frame any appellate
theory.

**Classification:** Standard. The difficulty prong is satisfied
(no clear path; Tim's personal strategic intelligence required).
The matter is not interesting in the doctrinal sense — it is
hard because the underlying record is bad, not because the legal
question is meaningful. Both Standard prongs are satisfied.

### Example 7 — Anti-SLAPP appeal, normal timeline → Preferred

**Facts:** DZ refers an anti-SLAPP appeal at the notice-of-appeal
stage. Briefing has not begun. The matter falls squarely within
KLG's anti-SLAPP practice area.

**Classification:** Preferred. Both prongs satisfied — normal
timeline AND interesting (anti-SLAPP is within KLG's core
practice values).

### Example 8 — Trust-and-probate appeal, urgent timeline → Standard

**Facts:** DZ refers a trust-and-probate appeal with an oral
argument set in 35 days. The legal issues are conventional
fiduciary-duty and accounting questions.

**Classification:** Standard. Urgent (35-day oral argument
window does not allow normal-pipeline preparation), and not
independently interesting (trust-and-probate is Yellow under
KLG's practice-alignment framework; conventional fiduciary-duty
issues do not qualify as interesting under prong (B)).

## Edge Cases

### Edge Case 1 — Brought in early but underlying record is broken

A matter brought in early enough for normal pipeline (prong (A)
satisfied), but the underlying record is so deficient that even
with the time available, Tim must personally invest substantial
strategic time to find a colorable issue.

**Resolution:** Preferred. The timeline qualifies the matter as
Preferred even if difficulty is high. The relationship rate
absorbs the difficulty in exchange for the runway.

### Edge Case 2 — Initially declined, then taken on DZ insistence

DZ has come back on a matter that Tim initially declined as
having no clear path. DZ insists. Tim agrees to take it.

**Resolution:** Standard. The initial decline reflects the
difficulty prong. Taking the matter on DZ's insistence does not
upgrade it to Preferred — DZ is paying for the work, not for
the friendship rate.

### Edge Case 3 — Mid-engagement change in difficulty or urgency

After the matter is classified and the engagement letter is
signed, the case develops in a way that changes the
classification (e.g., the appellate panel raises a constitutional
question that elevates the matter to "interesting"; or opposing
counsel takes a settlement off the table and an urgent writ
becomes necessary).

**Resolution:** The original classification governs the
engagement unless the engagement letter is amended. If the
matter materially shifts, the attorney decision is whether to
amend the engagement letter to reflect the new tier. Default is
to honor the original classification through the existing
deliverable, then re-classify at the next engagement (next brief,
next petition, etc.).

### Edge Case 4 — DZ requests Tim personally on a Preferred matter

DZ asks Tim to handle a matter personally rather than routing
through the firm, even though the matter classifies as Preferred.

**Resolution:** The Preferred tier is structured around the firm
performing the work. If DZ wants Tim personally on a recurring
or real-time basis on what would otherwise be a Preferred matter,
the matter converts to either Standard (with Tim doing most of
the hours at $650) or to the Consulting rate ($1,050) under the
KLG rate sheet. The choice is DZ's, made at intake. Tim should
not silently do the work himself at Preferred rates.
